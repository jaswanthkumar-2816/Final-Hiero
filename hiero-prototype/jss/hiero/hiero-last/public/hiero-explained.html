Hiero in two languages: technical and plain English

Left column is the product language. Right column is what a student should understand, with no jargon.

1. The student path

Technical Plain English
analysis.html → POST /api/analysis/analyze → localStorage.analysisResult → result.html Upload your resume and the job file. Hiero compares them and opens a results page.
result.html Start Skill Module → quiz.html (skill pick, then Beginner / Intermediate) Choose what you want to learn. Then say if you are new or already know some of it.
Beginner → learn.html?skill=…&track=beginner (no diagnostic) If you are new, skip the test. Watch videos and practice in order.
Intermediate → 10-question diagnostic → learn.html?…&track=intermediate If you already know some of it, take a short test. Then study only the parts you missed.
Suggested project → project.html?name=… Click a project idea to open the project workspace for that idea.
Start Mock Interview → mock-interview.html (role prefilled from analysis) Practice speaking for this job. The questions follow the job you uploaded.

2. What the results numbers mean

Technical Plain English
score (0–100), mostly JD requirement coverage. Extra coding skills do not inflate the score. How close your resume is to this job. Extra skills that the job does not ask for do not raise this number.
jobTitle parsed from the JD, ignoring campus placement noise The real job name, not the college registration or eligibility text.
domain e.g. tech, sales-core, non-tech What kind of job this is: software, sales, or something else.
matchedSkills Things this job needs that already appear on your resume.
missingSkills Things this job needs that are not on your resume. Learn these first.
extraSkills Skills you have that this job does not need. They are listed, but they do not raise the match score.
jdSkills / required skills The skill list Hiero used as the job’s real requirements.
skillToLearnFirst The single most important gap. Start here.
projectSuggestions[].title + reason A project you can build so a recruiter can see the missing skill, and why it helps this job.
atsScore How easy it is for a computer to read your resume (headings, verbs, length). This is not the job-match score.
interviewChance A rough guess of interview odds from the match score. A weak match stays low on purpose.

3. Buttons on the results page

Technical Plain English
Start Skill Module → quiz.html Pick a skill, then Beginner or Intermediate.
Missing-skill row → quiz.html?skill=… You already chose that skill. Next choose Beginner or Intermediate.
Suggested project card → project.html?name=… Open that project’s page (videos, GitHub, notes).
Start Mock Interview → mock-interview.html Start a practice interview for this job.
Re-Analyze → analysis.html Upload again with a new resume or a new job file.

4. Learning levels

Technical Plain English
track=beginner — sequential modules, curated videos, resources, problems. No 10Q quiz. You are starting from scratch. Follow the path in order. No test first.
track=intermediate — diagnostic 10Q, topic-gap clips on learn.html You already know some of it. Take 10 questions. Then watch only the parts you got wrong.
