The structural case

Why they can't follow

Every gap in this research has a cause.
None of them are oversights.

The obvious objection to Hiero is that each of its features exists somewhere else — so it looks like a regular product. The answer is not a longer feature list. It is that every competitor is prevented from closing these gaps by the thing that pays them. The gaps persist after everyone reads the same research, because fixing them would break the business underneath.

The category claim

What actually makes Hiero different

Three claims that survive the
"regular product" objection.
Claim 01 · Architecture

The loop is one system

Coursera teaches. HackerRank tests. Internshala lists jobs. Remasto mocks interviews. LinkedIn holds identity. Each owns a single slice and hands you off.

Diagnose → teach → re-test → prove → apply inside one product isn't a feature that can be copied in a sprint; it's an architecture. Internshala is the proof that it's hard — they own both learning and hiring and still never joined them.

Claim 02 · Incentive

The candidate is the customer

HackerRank, Internshala, Unstop and LinkedIn are all paid by the other side of the market. Everything a candidate needs — honest feedback, a reason for rejection, a second chance — is a cost line with no revenue attached.

Hiero is paid by the person being assessed. That single fact makes feedback a product rather than an expense, and it cannot be copied without changing who writes the cheque.

Claim 03 · Data

It measures capability, so it can prove it

Everyone matches résumés because the résumé is the only artifact they hold. CV Sync compares two documents. LinkedIn verifies identity. Udemy records that a video finished.

A proctored, graded, re-tested session is evidence of what someone can actually do — data no competitor is generating. Matching, ranking and proving all become possible downstream of it.

The honest condition on Claim 03 — and the reason reviewers see a regular product

Today nothing leaves Hiero that an employer can open and verify. Scores and tiers stay inside the app. Without that output, Hiero reads as mock interviews plus courses plus a job board — three things that exist everywhere, which is exactly why it looks ordinary from outside. The verifiable record is not one more feature on the roadmap; it is the thing that converts a feature list into a category position. Hiero already generates the raw material — proctored sessions, graded answers, evaluated solutions. What is missing is the credential and the public link that lets someone else check it.
